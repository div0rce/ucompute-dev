| D-019 | 2026-10-19 | **Unified compute credits with contributor consent**, a binding product and architectural principle (spec §26.5; economics model §29.4), as clarified by the owner the same day: "Use your own compute for free. Share compute to earn credits. Let trusted people use your hardware for free. Spend credits to access compensated compute. Purchase credits when you need more." and "Share your compute. Not your computer." One interoperable network and one user-facing compute-credit economy with one consumer-facing balance; internal accounting may distinguish transaction types. The hardware owner decides who may use it for free and who must compensate them. **Self-compute exclusion (binding):** users must not earn compute credits for processing workloads they originate themselves or for executing workloads on resources under the same beneficial ownership or economic control; local and private Mesh execution on personally controlled resources stay free of platform credit charges; credits are earned only through verified, authorized computing services delivered to an eligible independent consumer under a compensated arrangement. Separate usernames, accounts or device identities do not by themselves establish economic independence, and more devices, accounts or connections never by themselves generate credits; an organization serving independent external customers from several servers is legitimate. Controls are proportionate (authenticated execution records, transaction integrity, eligibility verification, fraud detection against self-dealing, circular and repeated artificial transactions, fabricated workloads or contributions, multiple accounts under common control, Sybil identities, manipulated metering, replayed accounting records, unauthorized issuance and double-spending); beneficial ownership cannot be established perfectly in a decentralized network, and the residual risk is recorded (threat model §6.2). **Sharing (owner-controlled, default off):** private only (the owner's workloads: 0 spent, 0 earned); donate (recipient 0, provider 0; donated-only capacity never sold or commercially allocated beyond the contributor's authorization); share for credits (the consumer spends per the applicable pricing, the provider earns per the agreed terms, transparent platform fee). The owner controls whether sharing is on, eligible requesting identities, permitted workload types, utilization limits, availability, commercial participation and revocation; changes to sharing or commercial eligibility are authenticated and authorized. **Trusted access (binding):** per-provider, identity-based free-access allowlists that coexist with compensated sharing on the same provider; for an allowlisted request consumer debit, provider reward and platform compute fee are 0 (operational metering only, no commercial credit transaction); free access takes precedence over that provider's pricing for eligible requests, and the requester is told before execution whether credits will be consumed. No silent paid fallback: if the allowlisted provider is unavailable the request waits, fails gracefully or uses another eligible free resource, unless the requester explicitly authorizes paid execution or configured a clear, bounded paid-fallback policy. Allowlisted identities are authenticated and stable (never usernames, display names, IP addresses, local network proximity or unauthenticated announcements) and each grant is scoped to that identity and provider; trusted access grants no host administration, file-system, shell, arbitrary-code, desktop-control, credential or network access beyond the workload's permissions, and needs no Cloud subscription. **Issuance and payment integrity:** Purchase → Spend → Execute → Verify → Compensate; credits become available only through confirmed purchases, eligible provider compensation (a transfer of a customer-funded spend, never new value and never duplicated) and explicitly budgeted, authorized grants; records cover purchases, balances, reservations, spending, compensation, platform fees, refunds, reversals and outstanding obligations, with invariants against duplicate issuance, double-spending, double compensation, unauthorized balance changes, self-compute rewards and unfunded commercial obligations. Honest providers are protected from inappropriate loss on chargebacks or failed collections through payment verification, fraud prevention, transaction exposure limits, reserves, risk-based settlement delays and clear provider terms; the platform is not assumed to absorb unlimited payment losses. **Credit value:** purchased credits represent prepaid access to eligible services under applicable terms and are never described as valueless; initially credits are not transferable between users and not automatically redeemable for cash; cash settlement needs a separately authorized process; credits never require cryptocurrency or blockchain, and none is implemented at this stage. These choices do not by themselves eliminate money-transmission, stored-value, consumer-protection or tax considerations; legal review settles them before any real-money function. No real payments, commercial settlement or payouts until owner authorization, security controls, legal and compliance review and operational readiness (spec §4, B-006); plain usage billing may precede purchasable prepaid balances (spec §26.5 stages). Self-hosted credit accounting, cross-network settlement, and ledger federation are outside the current committed scope. Their architecture and economic rules remain undecided. The 2026-10-19 engineering session's guardrails (backed issuance, no transfer or automatic cash redemption, sharing off by default, no real payments, settlement or payouts before authorization) are adopted in substance by this clarification; their detailed mechanisms in §26.5 change only by recorded owner decision. Free local Run, free private Mesh, self-hosted operation without UCompute-operated services or accounts, and open-source infrastructure are preserved; community access without payment remains possible through earned credits, donated capacity, fair-access mechanisms and trusted access, subject to actual capacity; Cloud is an optional managed service on the same interoperable infrastructure, never a mandatory intermediary for local execution or private Mesh; no competing credit currency or artificial network division. Prices, fees and the sustainability of the credit economy are hypotheses until the §29.4 model is supported by measurement. Scope: binds spec §§25–33, every implementation of the original rows and public materials; adds P1 row U-05 (M10); amends ADR-0004 decisions 3 and 7. Nothing of credits, payments, trusted access, Swarm or Cloud is implemented; all stay disabled, and specifying them starts no marketplace, billing or ledger work | Owner decision revision 2026-10-19; it replaces, before commit, the same-day draft ("Free Compute Commons") that barred community-contributed compute from paid work and so implied two economies. The owner's clarification of the same day, after commit bfcf4a7, adds the self-compute exclusion, trusted-access allowlists without silent paid fallback, issuance and payment integrity and the credit-value definition, and withdraws two points of the bfcf4a7 text: group-scoped self-hosted accounting (now outside committed scope, as recorded above) and the platform restoring every payment loss from its own funds (replaced by bounded exposure, reserves and provider terms). All 148 original rows and their evidence are preserved unchanged: F-07-02 (accounting), F-07-07 (credit ledger), F-07-08 (anti-Sybil), F-07-10 (price discovery), F-07-11 (gated settlement) and I-03 (reciprocity) now read as parts of one credit economy with consent-scoped contributor compensation. F-07-07's "no monetary value" statement holds literally in stage 0, where no money is in any path; once credits are purchased or usage-billed, the owner's credit-value definition governs the user-facing statement, an additive reconciliation that leaves the frozen row unchanged (§26.5). F-07-10/11's no-real-money criteria govern until the stage preconditions. Settlement and payouts stay gated (F-07-11, B-006, §4). Per-device consent and trusted-access scoping keep "share your compute, not your computer"; the self-compute exclusion and backed issuance keep earned credits a funded transfer for third-party work; no direct transfer or automatic cash-out is intended to reduce stored-value and money-transmission exposure; whether it does, including for the indirect requester-to-provider value path through compensated compute, is for counsel review under B-006 (not a legal opinion). Consistent with spec §3 (no mandatory cryptocurrency), §9 (no redeemable value without compliance and fraud controls) and §23 (open core not a crippled demo) |
